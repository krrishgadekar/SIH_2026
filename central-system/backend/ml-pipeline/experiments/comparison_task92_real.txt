--- THE HEAD-TO-HEAD: matched coverage ------------------------
    Both configurations answer 24 cases; both defer 28.
    Branch A defers its least-confident cases -- the strongest
    deferral rule a single-branch system has, so this is the
    baseline at its best, not a straw man.

--- Is disagreement a useful alarm? ---------------------------
    rule engine ran on 52 of 52 cases
    agreement 46.2%, disagreement 53.8% (28 cases)
    P(Branch A wrong)                  = 0.4423
    P(Branch A wrong | branches agree) = 0.2500
    P(Branch A wrong | disagreement)   = 0.6071
    LIFT                               = 1.3727

    Lift above 1 means disagreement concentrates real errors --
    the actual claim of the dual-branch design, and stronger
    evidence for it than any accuracy delta. At or below 1 the
    second branch is spending reviewer time at random.

--- VERDICT ---------------------------------------------------
    No difference is demonstrated: the confidence intervals overlap at matched coverage. The point estimates may differ, and that difference is not distinguishable from noise at this sample size. Do not claim the integrated pipeline wins.
    basis: overlapping 95% confidence intervals

Every figure describes THIS test split. Judged on confidence
intervals, not point estimates: two systems scored on a few
hundred images routinely differ by a point or two with fully
overlapping intervals, and calling that a win is how a
comparative claim turns out to be noise.
